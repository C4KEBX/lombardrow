# Script review: No. T-01 America's emergency oil, hidden in salt

**Score: 64/100**: The news peg is real and well sourced, but the hook gives the viewer no reason to care. The "caves of salt" tease is never paid off. The last question asks about gas prices, not the reveal.

Length check: 111 words of narration as written (hook 16, hormuz 14, left 15, embargo 17, ford 17, salt 16, today 16). Spoken numbers like "nineteen eighty-two" and "two hundred and eighty-three million" stretch that to roughly 38 to 42 s, inside the 30 to 45 s target. That leaves about 15 to 19 words of room, and the fixes below use it.

## The three fixes that would raise the score most

1. **Pay off the salt tease and the billion, about +6 points (Payoff, Story).** Scene `today`, as written: "Today it's 61 caverns in Texas and Louisiana. What are you paying for gas right now?" The hook promises "caves of salt" and the `ford` scene sets up "up to a billion barrels", but neither is paid off. The video ends on a site count, which reads like a list of facts. The repeatable line is right there in the facts: the reserve now holds under a third of what Ford's law aimed for, and salt is used because it doesn't let oil through. Rewrite: "Today it's 61 salt caverns in Texas and Louisiana. Salt doesn't let oil through. But the reserve holds under a third of Ford's billion." (23 words). Fact check: f-caverns gives "naturally impermeable underground salt caverns", "61 caverns" and the four sites in Texas and Louisiana. 283 million (f-spr-low) against "up to one billion" (f-epca) is 28 percent, which is under a third. Do not say "built for a billion": the law set a goal of up to a billion. The 714 million barrel capacity is only in the Rigzone corroboration, not a fact. To stay near 126 words, cut "on the Gulf Coast" from `salt` ("The government bought salt caverns. The first oil arrived in July 1977."). `today` already says where the caverns are.

2. **Put stakes and a present-day first frame in the hook, about +4 points (Hook, Picture plan).** Scene `hook`, as written: "America's emergency oil is at its lowest since 1982. And it's kept in caves of salt." The claim is concrete, but it is a stock level with nothing at stake for the viewer. The first frame also fights it: the 1973 Oregon pump sits under the title "An empty pump, 1973" next to the hook line "lowest since 1982". That puts two different years on screen in the first two seconds while the words describe today. Rewrite: "America's emergency oil just hit its lowest since 1982, during a Middle East war. And it's kept in caves of salt." (21 words). Fact check: f-release ties the release to "the Middle East war and the Strait of Hormuz crisis". Say "a Middle East war", not "a war", so it doesn't read as a US war. f-spr-low covers the week ending 9 Oct, released 15 Oct, so "just" holds. Do not add "oil over $100": it is in the brief but not in facts.json, so it needs a source first. Picture fixes: open on `spr-wellpad` (today), not `gas-empty`. Keep the empty pump for the cover only. Change the `hook` title to "Strategic Petroleum Reserve, Louisiana" or drop it. In `hormuz`, the title "The Strait of Hormuz" is still up when the shot cuts to `spr-pumps` at "releasing". If titles stay up for the whole scene, that labels Gulf Coast pumps as Hormuz. Move the title off, or end the scene on `hormuz-night`.

3. **End on the reveal with a question that loops, about +3 points (Ending).** Scene `today` and `meta.ending`, as written: "What are you paying for gas right now?" The question is easy to answer, but it has nothing to do with the reveal. Nothing in the script links the reserve to pump prices, and it doesn't flow back into the open. Rewrite the last line and the card as: "Refill it, or let it run down?" (7 words, `ending.atWord: "Refill"`). It comes straight off "under a third of Ford's billion". It loops into "America's emergency oil just hit its lowest since 1982". It invites argued, longer comments, which 2026 ranking rewards. It is a policy question, not financial advice. Keep the `gas-empty` shot under it for the loop to the cover.

With all three fixes, narration is about 126 words, which is about 44 to 47 s. That is inside the 20 to 60 s window. Drop "even" from "So why does it even exist?" if you need to tighten it.

## What works
- The news peg is fresh and primary-sourced: EIA weekly data for the week ending 9 Oct, released 15 Oct 2026. "Lowest since 1982" matches the fact exactly (the last lower week was 15 Oct 1982).
- `left` "So why does it even exist?" is a clean present-to-past hinge at about 12 s, which is the right moment to re-open the question.
- `embargo` "doubled, then quadrupled." has good rhythm and is word for word from the State Department source.
- `ford`: the kinetic text "Up to / a billion / barrels" over the actual signing-day photo (`ford-1975`, 22 Dec 1975) is a correct, strong picture-to-word match.
- The 1973 to 1974 Oregon pictures are all correctly dated and labelled "Oregon, 1973 to 1974". No group is captioned over the wrong people.
- The length is right for a trend short and leaves room for the fixes.

## Accuracy flags
- `salt` "The government bought salt caverns": f-first-oil says "acquired", and "bought" is more specific than the source. Use "acquired" or "took over", or drop the verb as in fix 1 ("The government's first oil arrived..." also works).
- `embargo` "Arab oil producers": the source says "Arab members of OPEC". That is acceptable shorthand, but "Arab OPEC members" is exact and the same length.
- `hormuz` "has been releasing it since March": f-release is the 11 March announcement. "Continues" release comes from the Rigzone headline (5 Oct). Supported, fine.
- `today` "Today it's 61 caverns": supported by f-caverns (BPC, secondary; CRS corroborates Texas and Louisiana).
- The brief's "oil over $100" has no fact in facts.json. Needs a source before it appears in narration or on screen.
- No line reads as financial advice.
