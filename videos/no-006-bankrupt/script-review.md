# Script review: No. 6 Why 'bankrupt' means 'broken bench'

**Score: 66/100**: The hook is strong, the facts are well hedged and the half-banknote reveal is very good, but a word detour stalls the story from about 6 s to 27 s. One line is broader than its source, and the closing question has already been answered by the time it is asked.

Word budget: the narration is 172 words with numerals counted as one word, or about 183 words spoken. It measured 68.5 s. Each fix below is word-neutral, so length and the 175-word floor stay as they are.

## The three fixes that would raise the score most

1. **Tighten the stretch from the hook to Perrott so the hanging story doesn't stall, about +4 points (Hook +2, Story +2).** At 3 s the viewer is promised "one London merchant did", but his name doesn't come until about 28 s. In between they hear an abstract tease, a myth that gets debunked, and a law. Make the tease concrete and move the turn line so it ends the myth scene as a re-hook, not as a dead stop.
   - `hook`, as written: "Even the word hides a break." (6). Rewrite: "Even the word 'bankrupt' hides something broken." (7). This names the word, which is said only once before, and makes the tease a picture without giving away "bench".
   - `myth`, as written: "Legend says a failed moneylender's bench was smashed. The dictionary says that's probably a figure of speech." (17). Rewrite: "Legend says a failed moneylender's bench was smashed. Dictionaries say it's probably just a figure of speech. The real record is darker." (21). The scene now ends on a hook ("darker") rather than on the debunk. Set the scene's microhook to "The real record is darker." It keeps the f-bench-myth hedge ("probably").
   - `law-1706`, as written: "The real record is darker. In 1706, England made hiding assets from creditors punishable by death. And that law hid one more idea." (23). Rewrite: "In 1706, England made hiding assets from creditors punishable by death. And that law hid one more idea." (18). Drop "The real record is darker." from its microhooks.
   - Net words: 0. `myth` and `law-1706` go from 17+23=40 to 21+18=39 (−1), and `hook` goes from 6 to 7 (+1).

2. **Make the ending a question that is still open, in plain words, about +3 points (Ending +2, Clarity +1).** `fresh-start` as written: "That other idea? Almost an afterthought: the discharge of debt, a reward for cooperating. The rope failed. The discharge lasted. So: the rope, or the reward. Which would keep you honest?" (31). By the time the question comes, the script has already said "The rope failed. The discharge lasted", so history has settled it and there is little left to argue about. "Discharge of debt" is also jargon a viewer has to work out. Rewrite (31): "That other idea? Almost an afterthought: cooperate, and your debts were wiped. The rope failed. The reward lasted. So, owing a fortune, which keeps you honest: the rope, or the reward?" This turns the debate into a personal one ("owing a fortune" calls back the £13,513), and ending on "reward?" loops back into "A bankrupt who hid his money could hang." Move the cue `atWord` from "discharge" to "wiped" (text "reward"), and move the backdrop shot's `atWord` from "discharge" to "wiped" too. Change `meta.ending.prompt` to "Owing a fortune: the rope or the reward?" and keep `atWord` "So". Checked against f-discharge: "almost as an afterthought", discharge as the incentive for cooperation, coercion "proved a failure", and discharge "would have a fruitful future", which supports "lasted". Nothing in it reads as financial advice.

3. **Fix the pictures that fight the words, about +3 points (Picture plan).** Word count is unchanged.
   - `law-1706`, at "death" (the on-screen cue "Hang"): the shot is `boe-note` at x0.5 y0.5 w0.25, which is the middle of the note: the large diagonal "SPECIMEN" stamp. Change it to `tyburn-1747` x0.62 y0.42 w0.22, the gallows.
   - `law-1706`, at "idea": the shot is `ludgate`, a street of shop fronts under "one more idea". Change it to `rake-fleet` x0.5 y0.5 w0.5. This shows the debtors' prison and sets up the backdrop of the ending.
   - `fresh-start`, at "lasted": the shot is `ludgate` again, now used for the third time. Change it to `bankers-1340` x0.8 y0.75 w0.35, the counting bench still whole, which also calls back to the word.
   - `trunk`: the Newgate crops at w0.15 and w0.18 take about 240 to 290 px of a 1600 px riot print and blow it up, so they will look soft. Use x0.68 y0.58 w0.28 on the barred window and wall, which still keeps out the 1780 fire and the rioters. At "1,000", x0.15 y0.62 lands on the mirrored back of the note and an arm of the SPECIMEN stamp. Use x0.12 y0.85 w0.3 on the torn lower edge, which reads as "half a note".
   - `hook`: if the scene title "A London hanging, by Hogarth" stays on screen while the shots cut to the banknote, Ludgate and the Italian bankers, those pictures are mislabeled. Check in the renderer that the title changes or hides when the `assetId` changes.

**Required regardless of score (see Accuracy flags):** in `four`, change "In 1820, the death penalty was abolished." to "In 1820, hanging for it was abolished." Both are 7 words.

## What works
- The open: "A bankrupt who hid his money could hang." Eight plain words, death as the stakes, and they match the cover and `meta.hookLine`.
- The hedges are kept: "Legend says", "probably a figure of speech", "the officials running his bankruptcy said", and "He was likely one of only 4", with the card reading "bankrupts likely hanged".
- `trunk` is real, specific and visual: "at the bottom of his trunk, tied in a rag".
- The loop set at `law-1706` ("one more idea") and paid at `fresh-start` ("That other idea?") gives the last 15 s something to do.
- The kinetic lines are short enough to read with the sound off: "Hide it. / Hang for it.", "Banca rotta. / Broken bench."

## Accuracy flags
- `four`: "In 1820, the death penalty was abolished." is broader than f-four-hanged, which says "the death penalty **for the crime** was abolished in 1820". As written it says England ended capital punishment in 1820, which is false. Change it to "In 1820, hanging for it was abolished." The on-screen callout "Abolished, 1820" is fine as it sits under the "bankrupts likely hanged" card.
- `trunk`: "halves of four banknotes" is acceptable, but the source says "the **other** halves", which implies the first halves were already accounted for. Don't strengthen this into "hidden halves" or "the stolen money". Any explanation of why the notes were halved needs a source.
- Every other line matches its fact. `f-1542` isn't used, which is fine.
