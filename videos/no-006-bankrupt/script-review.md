## What works
- "Hanged for hiding money" is the best hook line this video has had: a verb, a stake and a motive in four words, matched by the first spoken sentence.
- Splitting "About 3 million pounds today." into its own big-number scene gives the ear a clean number, after "13,513 pounds" is carried by the card.
- Moving "The rope failed. The deal lasted." into `deal` puts the thesis next to its evidence. With the fix 2 wording it is both quotable and accurate.
- The trunk now shows Newgate, the prison the source names, and the title is honest.
- The hedges hold: "officials said", "short, by the officials' count", "A witness swore", "likely one of only 4", "could be wiped", "usually freed", and "about, in today's money".

## Accuracy flags
- **None of the narration as written goes beyond its fact.** f-perrott-shop, f-deficiency, f-value-today, f-1706-capital, f-cut-notes, f-half-notes, f-perrott-hanged, f-four-hanged, f-discharge and f-discharge-today each cover their lines. "In England" is narrower than "England and Wales", so it is still true.
- **Implied link, watch it.** "So where was the money?" is followed straight away by the cut notes. Nothing in facts.json says those notes were the missing £13,513. The question is fine, but don't add any line or on-screen text such as "the missing money" or "his fortune, in halves". That would be stronger than the source.
- **Hook line and cover, "Hanged for hiding money".** f-perrott-hanged says he was hanged "for fraudulent bankruptcy", and f-1706-capital defines that as "hiding assets from creditors". "Money" is narrower than "assets", but the evidence was banknotes, so this is acceptable. f-discharge describes the capital crime as "refusal to cooperate". Don't strengthen the line to "for hiding £13,513" or anything similar.
- **"a way out they still get today".** f-1706-capital supports that the 1706 act introduced the discharge, and f-discharge-today supports discharge today. Nothing in facts.json says today's discharge comes from the 1706 act itself. The line as written ("a way out") is fine. "The same law still frees bankrupts" would need a source.
- **"The rope failed."** It is accurate as a paraphrase of f-discharge, but it can be read as a literal claim that the rope broke. Fix 2 removes that misreading.
- **Pictures, not claims.** The note crops show "17 83", a note from 22 years after Perrott, and "£ Seventy" under spoken figures of 13,513 and 1,000 (fix 1). `newgate-1780` shows the 1780 riot. Keep it off flames, so no one thinks the prison burned in Perrott's time.
- **Needs a source** before use: why Perrott was in Newgate (f-newgate), who Ferne was (f-ferne), and today's value of a £1,000 note from 1761.
