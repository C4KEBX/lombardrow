## What works
- The hook line is true and deadly, and it is the same on screen, in the cover and in the narration from frame one.
- "One London merchant did" pays off as "That merchant was John Perrott, of Ludgate Hill." That is a clean open loop, closed at about 28 s.
- The "hid / hides / Hide it. / Hang for it." motif ties the word, the law and the crime together. "Hide it. / Hang for it." is the best on-screen text in the script.
- `trunk` now keeps the source's own detail ("at the bottom of his trunk, tied in a rag: the other halves"). Without any comment, that shows he hid money.
- The hedges are kept in the narration: "probably a figure of speech", "what's considered", "Likely only 4".
- "Almost an afterthought" is a real twist with a source behind it, planted at about 27 s and paid off at the end.

## Accuracy flags

It is low risk. A safer version that is still in budget: "the commissioners said he was 13,513 pounds short" (+2 words, still at 187 after the fixes).
- `fresh-start`: "a fresh start" comes from plan.json's angle, not facts.json. Fix 3 removes it. If it stays, a fact defining discharge needs a source.
- `fresh-start`: "The discharge lasted." is on the strong side of f-discharge ("would have a fruitful future"). It is acceptable, but don't push it to "still exists today" (needs a source).
- `trunk`: why Perrott was in Newgate, and why the notes were cut in half, both need a source. Don't explain either without one.
- Picture: `tyburn-1747` is Tyburn and dated 1747. The narration says Smithfield and 1761, and the title must not claim otherwise (see fix 1).
- Otherwise none. Every narrated claim traces to a fact id in `speaks`.
