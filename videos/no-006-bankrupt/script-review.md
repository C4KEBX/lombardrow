## What works
- The hook line is true, deadly and plain, and it is the same in the narration, in the on-screen hook and in the cover from frame one.
- "One London merchant did" pays off as "That merchant was John Perrott, of Ludgate Hill." That is a clean open loop, closed at about 28 s.
- The "hid / hides / Hide it. / Hang for it." motif ties the word, the law and the crime together. "Hide it. / Hang for it." is the best on-screen text in the script.
- `trunk` shows the hiding without telling it: "at the bottom of his trunk, tied in a rag".
- The hedges are kept: "Legend says", "probably a figure of speech", "likely only 4", "said he was... short".
- `law-1542` now runs "Bankrupt? / Jailed." with matching cues, and the Hogarth arrest, then the Fleet prison, carry it.

## Accuracy flags
- `short`, card label "short in Perrott's books": this is stronger than f-deficiency, which is the commissioners' interrogatory *stating* a deficiency, not an audit of his books. Use "short, said the commissioners" (fix 2).
- `myth`: f-bench-myth is marked disputed. The Gazette corroboration ("the bench was broken in a symbolic show of failure") leans toward a real act, while Etymonline says "probably figurative". The narration correctly says "The dictionary says", so the claim is attributed. Keep it that way, and don't turn it into a flat "it never happened".
- `hanged`: the picture (Tyburn, 1747, engraved "Executed at Tyburn") must not appear as Smithfield 1761 (fix 1). The narration itself matches f-perrott-hanged.
- `fresh-start`: "The discharge lasted" (or "The reward lasted") is at the limit of f-discharge ("would have a fruitful future"). "Still exists today" or "a fresh start" would need a source.
- `trunk`: why Perrott was in Newgate, and where the first halves of the notes went, both need a source. Don't explain either without one.
- Otherwise none. Every narrated claim traces to a fact id in `speaks`.
