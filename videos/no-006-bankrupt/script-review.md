## What works
- The open line is concrete, deadly and short, and it matches the cover word for word except for "his".
- "Hide it. / Hang for it." works as a sound-off summary of the 1706 law.
- The `trunk` detail ("tied in a rag") is specific, visual and true to Kadens.
- The hedges hold: "Legend says", "probably", "officials said", "likely one of only 4", and the card labels "short, by the officials' count" and "bankrupts likely hanged, 1706 to 1820".
- "The rope failed. The reward lasted." is a quotable verdict that compresses f-discharge faithfully.
- The last shot returns to the cover image, which is the loop-frame pattern current guidance recommends.

## Accuracy flags
- Required changes: none. Every line matches its fact.
- Borderline, `fresh-start`: "cooperate, and your debts were wiped". In f-discharge, discharge is the incentive for cooperation. It doesn't say cooperation alone guaranteed it. A safer version is "your debts could be wiped" (+1 word, which needs a 1-word cut elsewhere).
- `short` → `trunk`: "Where was it?" followed by the notes invites the viewer to link them. Keep it implied. f-half-notes doesn't say the notes made up the £13,513, so don't add "found it" or "the missing money".
- Each of these needs a source before any line says it: why the notes were halved, how Perrott came to be in prison, and plan.json's angle that discharge is "the fresh start bankruptcy still means today".
- The fix 1 rewrites were checked: "probably not" keeps the "probably" hedge from f-bench-myth, and "outlived him" rests on f-discharge "fruitful future".
