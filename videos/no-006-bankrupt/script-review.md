# Script review: No. 6 Hanged for hiding his money

**Score: 74/100**: Buildable now. The detour is gone, the dates run in order, the payoff has its own scene and an on-screen number, and every line keeps its hedge. What still holds it back: the money trail stops one beat short of proof, the last question's "it" points at the wrong noun, and the opening number gives no sense of how much £13,513 was.

Word budget: 162 words as written, measured at about 57 s. The three fixes add 7 words (fix 1: +4, fix 2: +1, fix 3: +2), so it comes out at about 59 to 60 s. That is above the waived 45 s floor and below 70 s.

## The three fixes that would raise the score most

1. **Swap `cut` and `trunk` so that the trunk proves the witness right, about +4 points (Story +2, Payoff +1, Clarity +1).** As it stands, the viewer hears "halves of four banknotes" (`trunk`, about 22 to 28 s) before learning why anyone would cut a note. Then `cut` ends on "Ferne kept the matching halves." and the next line is the hanging. The evidence is never shown, so "So where was the money?" gets only half an answer. f-half-notes says the trunk held "the **other** halves", and f-cut-notes says searchers found halves of five notes at the lodging. In the source, that lodging is Ferne's ("At Ferne's, the searchers found halves of five banknotes", Credit Slips Part 3). So the click is already in facts.json. Put the witness first and let the trunk close it:
   - `cut` (now right after `law-1706`), as written: "A witness swore she saw Ferne cut banknotes in half and give one half to Perrott. Ferne kept the matching halves." (21) Rewrite: **"A witness swore she saw Ferne cut banknotes in half and give one half to Perrott. Searchers found halves of five notes at Ferne's."** (24, +3). Microhook: "Searchers found halves of five notes at Ferne's." Keep the lines "Half for him." / "Ferne's half.". Move the "Ferne's" cue from "kept" to the final "Ferne's".
   - `trunk` (now right before `hanged`), as written: "In his prison trunk, tied in a rag: halves of four banknotes, and half a 1,000 pound note." (18) Rewrite: **"In Perrott's prison trunk, tied in a rag: the other halves of four, and half a 1,000 pound note."** (19, +1). "Perrott's" replaces "his", because the last name spoken is now Ferne's, and the script never says whether Ferne is a man or a woman. Keep the microhooks.
   - Checked against the facts: f-cut-notes and f-half-notes (both Kadens, Credit Slips Part 3). Before building, change f-cut-notes in facts.json from "at her lodging" to "at Ferne's lodging". The source says this, and the current wording could be read as the witness's lodging. The order is now: the law, then the witness, then the search, then the match, then the rope. Each scene now leads into the next.

2. **Make the last question name the money, about +2 points (Ending +1, Clarity +1).** `fresh-start` as written: "…freed from their debts after 12 months. With the rope waiting, would you have hidden it?" The nearest noun before "it" is "their debts", and "12 months" is on screen, so a viewer hears "hidden their debts?". The CapCut piece below says comments showing confusion are a warning sign. Rewrite: **"With the rope waiting, would you have hidden the money?"** (10, +1). Update `meta.ending.prompt` to match. Now "money" echoes the first sentence ("hiding money from his creditors") word for word, and the Tyburn crop at "With" returns to the cover frame. The question also becomes a real argument, because "likely one of only 4 Englishmen" (`hanged`) tells viewers the odds were long. The question describes history and asks about a choice in 1760, so it is not financial advice.

3. **Give the hook concrete stakes, in words and on screen, about +2 points (Hook).** `hook` as written: "The hanging law also gave bankrupts something they still get today." "Something" is the one vague word in the open. Rewrite: **"The hanging law also gave bankrupts a way out they still get today."** (13, +2). Update the microhook to match. This promises a specific kind of answer without giving it away, and `deal` pays it off at about 42 s ("cooperate, and your debts could be wiped", f-discharge). On screen, `meta.hookLine` and `meta.cover.line`: "Hanged, £13,513 short" is now accurate, but in 2026 "£13,513" reads like a car loan. The size of the sum only arrives at about 14 s. Rewrite: **"Hanged, about £3m short in today's money"**. Keep the "Hanged" mark. Source: f-value-today ("about 3 million pounds"). Keep "about" and "in today's money". Without them, the line claims he was £3m short in 1760.

## What works
- The open still does the job: a hanging, a city and a motive in one sentence, then a reason to stay until the present day.
- Splitting `deal` from `fresh-start` worked. "almost an afterthought" is back, and "12 / months to be freed, usually" puts the best number on screen with its hedge.
- The hedges hold everywhere: "officials said", "short, by the officials' count", "likely one of only 4", "could be wiped", "usually freed".
- "A witness swore she saw Ferne…" fixes the round 1 accuracy problem and sounds better than the old line.
- The ending has a word echo ("hiding" / "hidden") and a matching frame (the Tyburn crop opens and closes the video). The payoff sits right before the loop point, which current guidance links to rewatches.

## Accuracy flags
- None in the narration as written. Every line traces to f-perrott-hanged, f-1706-capital, f-perrott-shop, f-deficiency, f-value-today, f-half-notes, f-cut-notes, f-four-hanged, f-discharge or f-discharge-today, with the source's hedges kept.
- facts.json wording: f-cut-notes says "searchers found halves of five notes at her lodging". The source says "At Ferne's, the searchers found halves of five banknotes". Correct the claim before using fix 1.
- Each of these needs a source before any line says it: why Perrott was already in Newgate in 1760, what Ferne was to Perrott, why the notes were halved, and whether the notes were part of the £13,513. The source also describes Ferne as a woman ("Mrs. Ferne"), but facts.json does not, so keep the script's ungendered wording unless that is added.
- Picture, not narration: the `short` opener shows the note's "1783" date (see Picture plan).
